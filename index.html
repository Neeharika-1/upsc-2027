    Mains-First
    Protocol

    January is now the revision-and-simulation month, not an artificial class-completion promise. Your listed backlog is 357 known class units before any uncounted work. With a hard cap of 3 classes a day and 18 full simulation days reserved, the earliest finish for that baseline is 23 February. The plan protects daily answer writing, newspaper, two current-affairs quizzes and 25 Prelims PYQs while revising in parallel.

    The thesis

    Three rules, no exceptions

    1 · Read for mains, drill for prelims

    Mains-level understanding covers roughly three quarters of what Prelims asks. Prelims also needs MCQ elimination skill and CSAT, which mains reading never trains, so those run as a separate weekly track from November. Mains-first is a content strategy. It is not permission to skip mock tests.

    2 · Nothing counts until it is written

    A topic is finished when you can reproduce it on a blank page from memory in 150 words. Highlighting, watching and "revising" are not output. Every study block ends with a recall or a written answer, even a bad one.

    3 · Every concept survives an attack

    Once a week, run one thought experiment: argue the opposite, remove an event from history, or take the officer's chair. Write 150–250 words, test your reasoning, and record one thing you learned. One deep experiment is enough; the rest of the week is for syllabus coverage, VidBodh answer writing and revision.

    Capacity check, not wishful thinking. 357 known classes ÷ 3 = 119 teaching days. Reserve 18 days for two full nine-paper Mains simulations (22–30 December and 22–30 January): the earliest finish for the 357-unit baseline is 23 February, assuming 3 classes on every other day, including Sundays. Any additional classes or missed class days push the date later. January is for the two simulations, rolling revision and error repair; the audited class queue and the first full-syllabus revision finish later if the arithmetic requires it.

    Confidence is not a feeling. It is a ledger of promises you kept. Fill the ledger.

    Patterns below come from the recurring advice in published topper interviews, not from any one person: one source per topic, many revisions, early answer writing, a short newspaper window, tests analysed harder than they are taken.

    One day, four templates

    Daily floor · all 7 days

    One newspaper session; two current-affairs quizzes; 25 Prelims PYQs; VidBodh answer-writing or model-answer work; brief class recall. That is 7 newspaper sessions, 14 quizzes, 175 PYQs and 7 answer-writing/review blocks each week.

    Weekly proof of work

    Hit the calculated class quota without exceeding 3 classes on any day; write 3 Sociology optional answers across the week; complete one thought experiment; analyse the Sunday PYQ set; update the Master Checklist and error log.

    Revision is parallel

    Every class ends with a short closed-book recall. Every Sunday, re-test the week’s weak areas. In January, spend the dedicated repair blocks on class notes and simulation errors. Do not postpone all revision until the final lecture is finished.

    Tick a week only when its class quota and output gate are complete. The quota is the maximum practical pace implied by your three-class cap, not a reason to binge four classes the next day if you fall behind. A missed class day moves the estimated finish date unless the audited backlog is lower.

    Source discipline

    Keep your chosen core sources. For each class unit, capture the syllabus heading, a short recall sheet, the relevant PYQ link, and one usable example or value-addition item. Do not turn class completion into a second full textbook pass.

    • One primary source per topic; use extra references only to repair a named gap.
    • Map Prelims PYQs and Mains PYQs to topics you have actually covered; revisit the error log weekly.
    • For Sociology, treat class notes as the base and practise applying concepts and thinkers to Indian examples.
    • Use the Master Checklist as the topic inventory, not as another source to read from beginning to end.
    The thought lab

    Make it interesting

    One experiment per week, ideally on Sunday: 30–45 minutes, 150–250 written words, then a short self-review. Draw one prompt, write before looking anything up, and log it only when you have completed the analysis. There are 24 prompts in the bank, so the bank supports about 24 weeks of deliberate practice—not multiple required experiments per week.

      The autopsy: for every question, tag the failure

      • K Knowledge gap. Add the fact to the weekly repair list.
      • S Structure. You knew it and arranged it badly. Rewrite the answer in 5 lines.
      • T Time. You ran over. Count minutes per question next time.
      • P Presentation. No diagram, no data point, no intro hook, no way forward.

      Tag every answer you wrote. The tag with the highest count is the next ten days of work. Don't read the model answer until you've tagged your own.

      Balance

      IGNOU, DAF, and the body

      MA Sociology (IGNOU)

      • It is your optional's foundation, not a second job. Budget about 6% of weekly hours, and write every assignment answer in mains format so one piece of work serves both.
      • Check the assignment deadlines and the next term-end exam dates on IGNOU's site this week. A June 2027 exam falls right after Prelims. Decide by 31 January whether to sit it or defer to the next cycle. The MA does not decide your rank.
      • Use IGNOU units for coverage, then one standard book for the thinkers and Indian society.

      DAF reading and writing

      • I am reading this as building real depth in your hobbies, interests, home state and background for the interview. The interview is likely to be early 2028, so this stays at about 4% until Mains is done.
      • One non-syllabus book every 3 weeks, tied to a DAF entry. Sunday: write 400 words on it as an opinion piece.
      • Keep a one-page file for your home district and state: economy, schemes, problems, one reform idea. Update it monthly.

      Newspaper + CA quizzes + PYQs

      • Newspaper every day for 40 minutes: fact, static link, significance. Stop when the timer ends.
      • Two current-affairs quizzes per day, about 15 minutes each. Capture only wrong and guessed answers in an error log.
      • 25 Prelims PYQs daily. Time the set; review wrong/guessed responses and classify the reason.

      VidBodh daily answer writing

      • Open the VidBodh Daily Answer Writing programme.
      • Through 30 December 2026, follow the programme's assigned 5/10/20-question days. On its non-writing days, consolidate your answers, compare model answers and read ahead; keep your own daily writing/review habit.
      • From 31 December, continue a self-directed daily GS answer from a PYQ and compare it against your notes/model answer. Simulation papers replace that day's additional GS set, not the review habit.

      Non-negotiables

      • Seven hours of sleep. A tired brain forgets what you studied that day.
      • 45 minutes of exercise, outdoors if possible. Treat it as exam prep.
      • No social feeds before 7 pm. Delete the apps on weekdays, not "limit" them.
      • Sundays are still class days (maximum three), with the weekly audit and one thought experiment. Keep the evening after the audit low-stimulation and protect sleep.
      • One win logged every night. Three missed days in a row triggers a Sunday reset.
      Gates · kill the weak plan early

      Targets, not predictions

      Full-length Prelims mocks, scored out of 200 on GS Paper I, with the real negative marking. Recent official cut-offs for the general category have sat roughly in the 75 to 90 band, so aim a clear 20 above it. If you miss a gate twice in a row, drop something (see the right column), not sleep.

      Mock score log

      Log every full Prelims mock or PYQ paper. Each score is compared with the band for its month, and the average of your last three shows the trend.
